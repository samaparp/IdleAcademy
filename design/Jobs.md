# Jobs and Titles

> [!abstract] Summary
> A **quest board** offers jobs. Completing one grants a **Job Title**.
> Titles give **permanent buffs**. The player picks which Title is
> **displayed**, purely for flavour.

## Quest board

- Unlocked by **default** — one of the three things available at the start.
- Offers **jobs**: objectives with rewards.
- Rewards include **[[Quid]]**, **Titles**, and unlocking features and
  locations.

> [!question] Naming
> "Union" was floated. Period alternatives, all more 1888 than "guild":
> **Society**, **Institute**, **Association**, **Bureau**, **the
> Exchange**, **Labour Exchange** (a real Victorian institution for
> matching workers to work). "Union" in 1888 Britain reads specifically as
> a **trade union** — fine if that is the intent, misleading if not.
>
> Whether this is the same thing as the Stennard **guild** in
> [[Stennard]], or a separate one, is undecided.

## Titles

- Earned by completing jobs.
- **Buffs are permanent** once the Title is earned.
- The Character tab's Title field is a **list of unlocked Titles**; the
  player switches which is shown.

> [!important] Owning vs displaying
> Buffs come from **owning** a Title, not from displaying it. Switching
> the displayed Title is **cosmetic only** and changes nothing mechanical.
> Otherwise the player would be choosing one buff at a time, which is a
> different system entirely.
>
> **Needs confirming.**

## Job paths

The first path is **writing**.

| Path        | First Title       | Notes                                |
| ----------- | ----------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Writer      | Novella writer    | Cosmic-horror flavour, a Lovecraft nod |

> [!note] Period reference points
> Lovecraft was born in **1890**, so a cosmic-horror novelist in 1888 is a
> nod rather than a reference. The period-correct weird-fiction names are
> **Poe** (d. 1849), **Le Fanu** (d. 1873), and **Arthur Machen**, who was
> writing in exactly this decade. Machen is the closest fit and is
> genuinely 1888.
